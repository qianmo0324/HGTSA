4. Experimental studies
4.1. Experiment design
In Section 4.2, a difficult instance is used to adjust the
parameters of HGTSA. Then, in Section4.3 the BRdata
instances are used to compare the number of neigh-
bourhood solutions generated by the hybrid neighbour-
hood structure in Section3.4.2. In Sections 4.4 through
4.6, the performance of HGTSA is rigorously evaluated
usingthreewidelyrecognisedtypesofFJSPbenchmarks:
BRdata (consistes of 10 instances), BCdata (consistes of
21 instances), and DPdata (consistes of 18 instances).
These benchmarks include instances of varying sizes
and complexities, providing a comprehensive testbed
for assessing the algorithm’s effectiveness. On these
benchmarks, HGTSA is compared with five state-of-art
algorithms for solving FJSP, hybrid genetic algorithm
(HA,proposedin2016)(LiandGao 2016),scattersearch
with path relinking algorithm (SSPR, proposed in 2015)
(González, Vela, and Varela2015), multi-start multi-
levelevolutionarylocalsearchalgorithm(GRASP-mELS,
proposed in 2017) (Kemmoé-Tchomté, Lamy, and Tch-
ernev 2017), master-apprentice evolutionary algorithm
(MAE, proposed in 2019) (Ding et al.2019)a n ds i n g l e -
solutionbasedlocalsearchalgorithmwithaninnovative
adaptiveweightingtechnique(AWLS,proposedin2024)
(Zhang et al.2024) to demonstrate the superiority and
effectiveness of HGTSA. For each instance, the formula
RE = 100 ×(UB
– LB) / LB is used to calculate the ‘rel-
ative error’, where LB denotes the lower bound and UB
denotes the upper bound. Subsequently, the Mean Rela-
tive Error (MRE) is used to compare the overall quality
of solutions obtained by different algorithms.
HGTSA is implemented in Java and executed on
an Intel(R) i9 Core computer with a 3.60 GHz CPU
and 16GB RAM. To eliminate the effect of random-
ness,allalgorithmsarerun10timesindependently.This
approach minimises the influence of randomness and
provides statistically significant comparisons between
algorithms.
